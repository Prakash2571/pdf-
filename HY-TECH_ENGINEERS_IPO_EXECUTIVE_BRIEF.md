> **Bottom line:** Hy-Tech is a profitable niche manufacturer offered at a defensible price, but it is not a clean governance or effortless growth story. Subscribe at ₹53; do not chase a listing price around ₹97.

## 1. Investment decision

| Investor action | Call | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| IPO application at ₹50–53 | **Subscribe** | 22.3x FY26 earnings for a ~22% EBITDA-margin, ~20% ROE business with low post-issue leverage. |
| Listing-day purchase above ₹85 | **Avoid** | At ₹97, P/E rises to 38.5x and EV/EBITDA to 20.9x; full capacity execution is pre-paid. |
| Three-year investor | **Accumulate below ₹70** | Fair value ₹75–85 assumes the expanded capacity reaches ~65–70% utilisation and margins hold. |
| If allotted | **Part-book above ₹85** | Retain only if utilisation rises, job-work intensity falls and the US channel remains stable. |

### Why the issue is investable

- **Established operating franchise:** 48 years in hydraulic fittings, over 10,000 SKUs, six plants, and IATF 16949 certification at three units.
- **Strong unit economics:** FY25 gross margin 65.4%, EBITDA margin 22.2%, ROE 21.4% and ROCE 20.5%.
- **Sticky customer behaviour:** 95.2% of FY25 revenue came from repeat customers; top-10 concentration declined from 53.6% in FY23 to 42.0% in FY25.
- **Real integration:** captive forging plus machining, testing and proposed plating support margins and qualification barriers.
- **Manageable financial risk:** FY25 net debt/EBITDA was 0.72x; ₹160 million of IPO proceeds is intended for debt repayment.

### Why the valuation must remain discounted

- Every rupee of US revenue is sold through or sourced by **Hy-Tech USA Inc., a promoter-group entity**; the channel represented 22.85% of FY25 revenue.
- FY23–FY25 revenue rose 21%, but EBIT increased only 1.9% while operating invested capital rose 50.7%.
- Existing capacity was only ~63–70% utilised before a proposed ~60% addition.
- Cash conversion weakened sharply: CFO/EBITDA fell from 99.0% to 47.5% between FY23 and FY25.
- Product-wise revenue, true OEM share and end markets for 39.8% of revenue are not adequately disclosed.

## 3. Business, revenue and customer quality

| Attribute | Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Product | DIN, JIC, ORFS, conversion and customised hydraulic fittings; >10,000 SKUs | High criticality and qualification value, but product mix revenue is undisclosed. |
| Revenue quality | 96.2% product sales; point-in-time recognition; trivial contract liabilities | **Strong accounting quality** with little revenue-recognition judgement. |
| Repeat business | 95.2% of FY25 revenue; 128 repeat customers | **Strong behavioural stickiness**, but no long-term contracts. |
| Customer concentration | Largest customer 7.82%; top 10 at 42.02% | Improving, although four top-10 customers are unnamed. |
| Geography | Domestic 71.7%; exports 28.3%; USA 22.85% of total revenue | Domestic growth offsets a concentrated US export franchise. |
| End markets | Construction, farming and automotive = 52.3% | Cyclical and correlated; 39.8% remains classified as “Others.” |
| Channel | Direct 88.6%; distributor 11.4% | “Direct” is not the same as OEM; true OEM revenue is not quantified. |

### The US related-party issue

Hy-Tech USA is the exclusive distributor for North America, Canada and Brazil. Direct sales to it plus customers sourced by it equalled **100% of Company US revenue** in FY23–FY25. Combined exposure was ₹368.69 million, or **22.85% of FY25 revenue**. Nearly all sales commission is paid to this promoter entity.

This creates three risks: minority investors own the manufacturer but not the offshore distribution margin; the Company has no independent route to its largest export market; and tariff or pricing pain can potentially be allocated between related parties. This does not invalidate the IPO at 22x earnings, but it warrants a permanent governance discount.

### Industry position

The Indian hydraulic fittings market is projected to grow at **10.4% CAGR during CY24–CY30** and is over 90% unorganised. Fragmentation pressures commodity pricing, while IATF certification, broad SKU capability and export qualification protect organised suppliers. Hy-Tech's ~22% EBITDA margin is materially above the disclosed unlisted fittings peers, but standalone pricing power is limited: FY24 revenue grew 3.3% while EBIT fell 39.6% as cost pass-through lagged.

## 4. Operations, margins and cash conversion

### Capacity and bottlenecks

| Operating measure | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | Read-through |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Fittings production (lakh pieces) | 247 | 248 | 288 | FY25 growth was volume-led. |
| Year-end capacity (lakh pieces) | 326 | 366 | 455 | Capacity expanded ahead of demand. |
| Calculated utilisation | 75.8% | 67.8% | **63.3%** | Down two years; reported KPI uses a higher undisclosed denominator basis. |
| Job-work charges / revenue | 7.00% | 7.99% | **7.84%** | Outsourced machining signals an internal constraint. |

The IPO adds approximately **288 lakh pieces**, taking FY26 capacity from about 483 lakh to roughly 771 lakh pieces. The primary bottleneck is **CNC machining**, evidenced by rising outsourced job work, WIP and a capex list dominated by CNC equipment. The secondary bottleneck is **plating**; its Consent to Operate had not yet been applied for at the DRHP date. Post-expansion utilisation initially falls to ~44%, so absorption is a three-to-five-year task on industry growth alone.

Three-year cumulative CFO was ₹667.06 million against ₹575.92 million of capex; free-cash-flow conversion was only **18.5% of cumulative PAT**. Core trade working capital reached 34.6% of revenue, while the cash conversion cycle rose from 12 to 65 days, mainly because supplier credit normalised.

## 5. Valuation, risks and decision triggers

### Price sensitivity

| Price | Market cap (₹cr) | FY26 P/E | EV/EBITDA | View |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **₹53** | **503** | **22.3x** | **12.2x** | Attractive relative to quality and execution optionality. |
| ₹75 | 686 | 30.4x | 16.5x | Near the lower end of three-year value. |
| **₹97** | **870** | **38.5x** | **20.9x** | Full execution priced in; avoid chasing. |

At 60–75% utilisation of the expanded base, 22% EBITDA margin and 12x EV/EBITDA, estimated value ranges from approximately **₹71 to ₹89 per share**. A reasonable three-year fair-value range is **₹75–85**. The valuation case fails if margins settle below 20%, utilisation remains weak, or working-capital absorption prevents cash generation.

### Highest-priority risks

1. **Related-party channel:** promoter-owned Hy-Tech USA controls the route to ~23% of revenue.
2. **Capital productivity:** FY23–FY25 EBIT was nearly flat despite 50.7% more operating invested capital.
3. **Expansion absorption:** ~60% capacity addition against only ~63–70% current utilisation.
4. **Cash conversion:** CFO/EBITDA fell to 47.5%; cumulative FCF/PAT was 18.5%.
5. **Disclosure gaps:** no product-family revenue, no true OEM split, and unidentified end markets for 39.8% of sales.
6. **Execution/regulatory:** plating approval and commissioning can delay throughput from the new CNC assets.
7. **Cyclicality and tariffs:** correlated construction/agriculture/automotive demand plus concentrated US exposure.

### What clients should monitor quarterly

- Unit-level capacity utilisation and revenue growth relative to installed capacity.
- **Job-work charges as a percentage of revenue** — this should decline after CNC commissioning.
- EBITDA margin around the 20–22% range and ROIC moving back toward 20%.
- Receivable days, core working capital and CFO/EBITDA recovery above 70%.
- Any change in Hy-Tech USA commission, agreement, related-party balances or tariff allocation.
- Consent to Operate and commercial ramp-up of the plating facility.

> **Final call:** Apply at ₹53, avoid buying near ₹97, and use ₹75–85 as a three-year fair-value framework rather than a guaranteed target. The company has real manufacturing quality; the required discount reflects related-party dependence, weak recent capital productivity and execution risk.
