## 1. Investment view

**On the fundamentals at the issue price, this is a reasonably priced, genuinely profitable small-cap engineering business. On the grey-market price it is not.**

The two-part conclusion matters because these are two different decisions:

**(a) Subscribing at ₹50–53 — defensible.** You are buying a 48-year-old, ISO/IATF-certified, backward-integrated precision machining business at 22.3x trailing earnings and 12.2x EV/EBITDA, with a 22% EBITDA margin, 21% ROE, 0.25x net debt/equity, 96% of revenue from repeat customers, and a funded plan to add ~60% to capacity. That is a below-market multiple for those characteristics. The asymmetry is favourable *at this price*.

**(b) Buying on listing near the GMP-implied ~₹97 — not defensible.** At ₹97 the stock trades at 38.5x FY26 and 20.9x EV/EBITDA. My capacity-based fair value ladder (Section 12) puts full, successful execution of the entire expansion — 70–75% utilisation on a 771-lakh-piece base, i.e. revenue of ₹3.0–3.2 bn, roughly **+60–70% from FY26** — at ₹83–89/share on 12x EV/EBITDA. In other words, **₹97 already discounts more than a complete, on-time, on-budget execution of a project that has not yet started.** There is no margin of safety left for the structural issues below.

### The three things that decide this investment

1. **Roughly 23–27% of revenue is intermediated by a promoter-owned company that minority shareholders will not own.** Hy-Tech USA Inc. is a Promoter Group entity and the exclusive distributor for North America, Canada and Brazil. Direct sales to it *plus* customers it sources equal **100.0% of the Company's US revenue in each of FY23, FY24 and FY25** (₹368.69 mn / ₹338.25 mn / ₹356.14 mn). All of the Company's sales commission (₹17.05 mn in FY25) is paid to it. This is a permanent related-party dependency sitting on the single largest export market, and it is the most important non-financial fact in the document.

2. **EBIT was flat for two years while invested capital rose 50%.** FY23 EBIT ₹252.04 mn → FY25 EBIT ₹256.81 mn (**+1.9%**) on revenue +21.0%. Over the same period operating invested capital went from ₹810 mn to ₹1,221 mn (**+50.7%**) and ROIC fell from 23.2% to 15.8%. FY25's optical 69% PAT growth is a recovery off a depressed FY24, not a new level. The bull case rests entirely on this pattern *not* repeating with the new capex.

3. **The document has real internal inconsistencies.** Five are material enough to name (Section 13), including an industry-report EBITDA table that double-deducts other income, a customer-concentration claim that compares top-5 against top-10, and a Weighted Average Cost of Acquisition that appears not to be adjusted for the 1:2 sub-division and ~112:1 bonus issue — overstating WACA by ~16% and thereby flattering the pricing optics. None of these change the audited numbers. All of them are a read on disclosure discipline in a first-time issuer with a sole, small BRLM.

## 2. The business in one page

Hy-Tech Engineers (incorporated 1978, Thane) manufactures **hydraulic fittings** — the connectors that join pipes, tubes and hoses inside high-pressure hydraulic circuits. These are low-ticket, high-criticality, high-SKU-count precision machined parts: a leak in a fitting takes down the whole machine, so once a fitting is designed into an OEM's platform it is rarely re-tendered on price alone.

**Portfolio:** >10,000 SKUs at FY25 (>11,000 per RHP) across four standard families — DIN metric, JIC flared/flareless, O-Ring Face Seal (ORFS), and conversion fittings — plus customised fittings. New SKU additions: 1,538 (FY23), 2,206 (FY24), 1,676 (FY25).

**Six plants**, four in Maharashtra and two in Madhya Pradesh, 32,887 sq m:

| Unit | Capability | Installed capacity (31-Mar-25) |
|---|---|---|
| Thane, MH | Hydraulic fittings | 35.00 lakh pc p.a. |
| Shirwal (Satara), MH | Hydraulic fittings | 144.00 lakh pc p.a. |
| Kavathe (Satara), MH | Hydraulic fittings | 180.00 lakh pc p.a. |
| Pithampur-I, MP | Hydraulic fittings | 48.00 lakh pc p.a. |
| Pithampur-II, MP | Hydraulic fittings | 48.00 lakh pc p.a. |
| Nashik, MH | **Forged metal parts** (mostly captive) | 3,120 MTPA |

**Total 455 lakh pieces p.a. + 3,120 MTPA forging** at FY25; **483 lakh pieces** at FY26.

**Vertical integration** is the real structural asset: Nashik forging feeds the machining units captively, and the IPO adds a dedicated plating plant. Forging → machining → plating → testing in-house is unusual at this revenue scale and is what supports a 65% gross margin.

**Certifications:** ISO 9001 (Shirwal, Nashik, Thane, Kavathe); ISO 14001 + ISO 45001 (Shirwal); **IATF 16949** (Shirwal, Pithampur-I, Pithampur-II) — the automotive qualification, and a genuine barrier to entry against the unorganised segment.

**People:** 425 permanent + 260 contract (38% contract) at FY25. Sales & marketing: 13 people. **Business is B2B, purchase-order based, with no long-term contracts.**

## 3. Industry structure

### The market
- Indian hydraulic fittings market: **7.7% CAGR CY19–CY24**, projected **10.4% CAGR CY24–CY30** (CARE Edge / Maia Research).
- Global: USA 6.0% → 8.2%; Europe 6.3% → 8.6%; Asia 6.9% → 9.3%; Middle East 7.0% → 10.2%.
- **Disclosure limitation:** every absolute market-size chart in the Industry Overview (pages 142–153) is embedded as a raster image with no extractable text. I can confirm the growth rates but **cannot verify the Company's absolute market share** from the document. Treat any "% market share" claim from other sources as unsourced.

### Structure: fragmented, and that is the whole thesis
Per the CARE Report, the Indian industry is **dominated by SMEs with only a few large players, and more than 90% of the industry is unorganised**, with high competitive intensity.

This cuts two ways, and the direction depends entirely on the customer:

- **Where it helps:** an unorganised workshop cannot hold IATF 16949, cannot run 10,000+ SKUs, cannot serve an export customer's PPAP requirements, and cannot absorb a forging-to-plating integrated cost structure. In the OEM and export channel, Hy-Tech is not competing with the 90% — it is competing with a handful of organised peers. This is why gross margin is 65% and not 25%.
- **Where it hurts:** in the domestic distributor/replacement channel, the unorganised 90% sets the price. And the FY26 growth is coming disproportionately from domestic (Section 6), which is the more price-exposed channel.

**Five Forces (my assessment):**

| Force | Intensity | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | **High** | >90% unorganised, low differentiation in standard SKUs. Mitigated only in qualified OEM/export work. |
| Buyer power | **High** | No long-term contracts anywhere. Top-10 = 42% of revenue. Purchase-order basis, terms revisable at will. |
| Supplier power | **Low–Moderate** | Steel is a liquid commodity; top-5 supplier share cut from 55.3% to 38.5%. But no purchase agreements, so no price protection either. |
| Substitution | **Low** | Hydraulics is designed-in. Long-term electrification of off-highway equipment is a 10-year-plus risk, not a 3-year one. |
| New entrants | **Moderate** | Trivial at workshop level; hard at IATF/export level. ₹300 mn buys meaningful capacity — this is not a capital-intensive moat. |

### Trade flows
India's fittings exports (HSN 73079990): top-5 countries = 67%, **USA alone 40%**. Imports: top-5 = 68%, **China 25%**. So the sector's export engine is heavily US-levered and its import competition is heavily China-levered — precisely the two axes most exposed to the 2025–26 tariff realignment. As of early 2026 the US reciprocal tariff on India was reduced from 25% to 18% and subsequently to a blanket 10% temporary levy, while **Section 232 metals tariffs of ~50% on steel/aluminium and ~25% on many downstream metal products remain in force**, and there is a standing **countervailing duty order on forged steel fittings from India** (sunset review concluded April 2026). Sources: [US Embassy India trade fact sheet](https://in.usembassy.gov/fact-sheet-the-united-states-and-india-announce-historic-trade-deal/), [Indian Express on the 10% levy](https://indianexpress.com/article/business/us-supreme-court-strikes-down-trump-tariffs-india-trade-impact-explained-10544914/), [Economic Times on Section 232](http://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/economy/foreign-trade/us-tweaks-metal-tariffs-but-keeps-50-percent-duties-on-steel-aluminium-copper-imports/articleshow/131465502.cms), [Federal Register CVD sunset review](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/04/15/2026-07312/forged-steel-fittings-from-india-final-results-of-the-expedited-first-sunset-review-of-the). *Content rephrased for compliance with licensing restrictions.* **Investors must confirm the tariff line applicable to the Company's specific HS codes — the DRHP predates all of this and does not address it.**

### 4.2 The margin story is a V, not a trend

**FY24 was the accident; FY25 was the recovery; neither is the run-rate.** FY24 EBITDA margin collapsed 710 bps on just +3.3% revenue, from three simultaneous hits:
- materials cost rose to 36.1% of revenue from 34.8% — the Company attributes this to "higher procurement rates and variation in the product mix";
- employee cost +16.7% (₹27.56 mn) against +3.3% revenue — hiring ahead of the Kavathe ramp;
- other expenses +38.7%, of which ₹16.03 mn bad debts and ₹4.00 mn investment write-off.

FY25 reversed all three. But look at the level, not the direction: **FY25 EBITDA margin of 22.2% is still 130 bps below FY23's 23.5%, and FY25 EBIT margin of 15.9% is 300 bps below FY23's 18.9%.** The gap is depreciation: D&A went from 4.6% of revenue to 6.3%. That is the permanent cost of the capex already spent, and the IPO adds more.

### 4.3 Earnings quality — the FY25 PAT is ~10% lower than it looks

The **₹26.88 mn gain on asset disposal is 10.3% of PBT and 13.7% of PAT.** From the PPE note: gross disposals ₹31.44 mn less accumulated depreciation ₹17.36 mn = **net book value ₹14.08 mn, sold for ₹38.32 mn — 2.7x book.** The composition tells you why: ₹4.06 mn of the disposal was *leasehold land and building carrying zero accumulated depreciation*. This is a property gain, and it is not repeatable.

**Normalising FY25 PAT to ₹176.17 mn cuts the growth rate from +69.2% to +51.7%, and the PAT margin from 12.2% to 10.9%.** That is the number to underwrite.

Two further quality notes:

- **Scrap sales** of ₹51.09 mn are 3.2% of revenue and **8.6% of raw material consumed** — a high but stable ratio, consistent with bar-turning/machining-intensive production. It is genuine operating revenue, but it is a steel-price-linked, not a value-added, line.

### Volume versus price — the crucial split

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|

This is the single most useful table I built, because it separates two completely different years:
- **FY24 growth was 100% price, 0% volume.** Volumes were flat; the +3.3% revenue came from realisation. And realisation gains did *not* hold margin — EBITDA margin fell 710 bps. That tells you the price increase was **cost pass-through, not pricing power**, and it was incomplete.

**Read-through:** the business has **volume-driven operating leverage but weak standalone pricing power.** In a steel up-cycle it will lag on margin; in a volume up-cycle it will beat. Given >90% unorganised competition, that is exactly what you would expect, and it is a permanent characteristic — not a phase.

## 6. Product mix analysis

**This is the weakest disclosure in the document, and clients should know it.**

The DRHP describes four product families (DIN metric, JIC flared/flareless, ORFS, conversion), two taxonomies (by connection type: compression / crimp / flange / push-to-connect / end / threaded; by function: tee / cross / elbow / sleeve / cap / adapter / coupling / plug / union / nipple / valve), and >10,000 SKUs. **It discloses revenue for none of them.** There is no product-wise revenue table, no product-wise margin, no standard-versus-customised split in rupees.

What can be inferred:

| Inference | Basis | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Standard catalogue fittings dominate | 10,000+ SKUs, DIN/JIC/ORFS/NPT/BSP standards-based; distributor channel exists | High |
| Customisation is a growing but unquantified share | 1,676 new SKUs in FY25; DRHP and CARE both flag customised fittings as the higher-margin growth vector | Moderate |
| Mix is skewed to straight and forged fittings | Process flow describes two distinct routes — "straight fittings" via multi-spindle blanking + centreless grinding, "forged fittings" via hot forging + **outsourced** rough machining | Moderate |
| Forging output is largely internal | "Majority of the products forged at this Unit are utilized by us for captive consumption" | High |
| No single product family is a margin cliff | Blended gross margin held at 63.9–65.4% across three years despite mix shifts | Moderate |

**Why the gap matters commercially:** in fittings, ORFS and customised high-pressure parts carry materially better margins than commodity adapters and nipples. Without the split, an investor **cannot tell whether the 22% EBITDA margin is a rich-mix outcome that could dilute as volume scales into commodity SKUs, or a structural process-efficiency outcome that will hold.** The entire post-expansion margin assumption in every model — including mine — is therefore unverifiable from the document.

**Ask management directly:** revenue and gross margin split by (i) standard vs customised, (ii) the four product families, (iii) forged vs bar-stock route.

**Every rupee of US revenue in all three years runs through or is sourced by the promoter-owned entity.** The exact arithmetic tie to total USA revenue in each year is not a coincidence — it confirms there is no independent US channel.

Related-party economics:
- **Commission expense to Hy-Tech USA: ₹17.05 mn (FY25), ₹14.07 mn (FY24), ₹14.50 mn (FY23).** Total "commission on sales" in other expenses is ₹17.05 / ₹14.07 / ₹15.47 mn — meaning **essentially 100% of the Company's sales commission is paid to the promoter entity.**
- Receivable from Hy-Tech USA: ₹20.86 mn; commission payable to it: ₹7.46 mn (FY25).
- Promoter loans of ₹40.00 mn (Hemant ₹25 mn, Surekha ₹15 mn) remain outstanding, unchanged across all three years, at ~10% interest (₹4.00 mn p.a.) and classified as >5-year maturity.

**Why this is a first-order issue, not a footnote:**

1. **Value is split outside the listed entity.** Minority shareholders buy the manufacturer; the promoters separately own the US distribution margin. The commission rate is the visible transfer, but a distributor's economics are the *spread*, not the commission — and that spread is invisible to public shareholders.
2. **It is structural and permanent**, not a legacy item being unwound. The exclusive agreement is presented as a strength; there is no stated intent to acquire or internalise it.
3. **It is unhedged single-point risk on 23% of revenue.** The DRHP itself concedes that operational inefficiency, management change, or financial instability at Hy-Tech USA would hit sales — and the Company has no alternative US route.
4. **It creates an incentive asymmetry** on where US pricing sits, especially under tariffs: who absorbs a US duty — the listed manufacturer or the promoter-owned distributor?

**This is not a reason to avoid the issue at 22x. It is a reason to require a discount to peers rather than parity, and to refuse to pay 38x.**

*(One more disclosure slip here: the DRHP states revenue from Hy-Tech-USA-sourced customers "increased at a CAGR of 14% from ₹279.85 million in Fiscal 2023 to ₹319.59 million." ₹279.85 → ₹319.59 over two years is **+14.2% cumulative — a 6.9% CAGR**, not a 14% CAGR.)*

## 10. Bottleneck process analysis

The disclosed process flow: **RM procurement & inspection → hot forging (Nashik, captive) → blanking on multi-spindle machines (straight fittings) / rough machining OUTSOURCED to subcontractors (forged fittings) → centreless grinding → thread rolling → CNC machining → crack testing → plating → assembly, packaging & dispatch.**

The Company follows a **cell-based manufacturing model** — small self-contained cells, some dedicated to specific customers or product categories. Good for mix flexibility and accountability; structurally *worse* for aggregate utilisation, because dedicating cells to customers strands capacity when any one customer's volume dips. **This partly explains why utilisation sits at 63–70% rather than 85%.**

### The bottleneck is CNC machining — five independent pieces of evidence

**1. Job-work charges are the smoking gun.** The Company outsources rough machining because it cannot do it all in-house.

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job work charges (₹mn) | 93.36 | 110.01 | **126.51** |
| % of manufacturing expenses | 30.6% | 32.1% | **32.8%** |
| **% of revenue** | **7.00%** | **7.99%** | **7.84%** |

Job work is the **single largest manufacturing cost line**, grew **+35.5% versus revenue +21.0%**, and rose from 7.00% to 7.84% of revenue. The DRHP states plainly that this reflects *"partial outsourcing of machining activities to manage capacity and lead times."* **Outsourcing intensity increasing faster than revenue is the definition of a binding internal constraint.**

**2. The capex is ~65% CNC machining.** ~₹121 mn of the identifiable equipment list is CNC lathes and sliding-head machines. Management is spending where the constraint is.

**3. Rising WIP inventory.** WIP: ₹66.49 → ₹71.90 → ₹87.95 → **₹98.58 mn.** WIP days 19.7 → 23.3 → 22.3. **WIP is now 36.9% of total inventory.** Accumulating WIP in front of a process step is the classic queueing signature of a capacity-constrained operation.

**4. Contract labour at 20% of manufacturing cost.** ₹58.17 → ₹70.25 → ₹77.03 mn, and 260 contract workers against 425 permanent (38%). Labour is being flexed around machine availability.

**5. Vertical storage systems are being bought** for Pithampur-I and Shirwal — a material-handling and WIP-staging constraint, consistent with points 3 and 4.

### The secondary bottleneck is plating — and it carries regulatory risk

Plating sits **immediately before assembly and dispatch**, is inherently a batch process, is environmentally regulated, and is currently not present at every unit. The IPO establishes a **dedicated plating plant at Pithampur.**

This is the highest-risk item in the capex, for a specific reason: **the *Consent to Operate* from the Pollution Control Board is disclosed as "Yet to apply."** A plating line cannot legally run without it. Only *Consent to Establish* has been received. Electroplating consents are among the more contested categories (effluent, heavy metals). A delay here would idle the CNC investment behind it, because plated parts cannot ship. **This is a single-point regulatory dependency on the throughput of the whole expansion, and it is not flagged as a headline risk in the DRHP.**

**Tertiary constraint — testing and validation:** crack testing, impulse test stands, hydrostatic benches and metallurgical labs are being added at all three units. For an IATF 16949 supplier, testing throughput gates *qualification of new SKUs* (1,676 added in FY25), not just production. This is a growth-rate constraint rather than a volume constraint.

### Bottleneck map

| Rank | Process step | Evidence | IPO addresses? | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **CNC machining** | Job work 7.8% of revenue and rising; WIP building; ~65% of capex | ✅ Directly (~₹121 mn) | Commissioning + skilled-operator ramp |
| 2 | **Plating** | Batch process before dispatch; new dedicated plant | ✅ New plant | 🔴 **Consent to Operate not yet applied for** |
| 3 | Testing / validation | Test benches, impulse stand, metallurgy at all 3 units | ✅ | Low |
| 4 | WIP staging / material handling | WIP 36.9% of inventory; vertical storage being bought | ✅ | Low |
| 5 | Forging (Nashik) | 59.6% utilised — **not** a constraint | ➖ Not needed | None near-term |
| 6 | Skilled machinists | 38% contract labour; 425 permanent | ❌ Not addressed | Moderate — attrition/training on new CNC |

**Verdict: the diagnosis is right and the capex is aimed correctly.** The measurable success test is simple and public — **job-work charges should fall as a percentage of revenue from FY28.** If they do not, the debottlenecking failed. This is the single best KPI to track post-listing, and it is disclosed in the notes to accounts every year.

(The Company's own CARE-sourced table shows a WC cycle of 125 → 131 → 143 days on a different definition; both point the same way.)

**The driver is not receivables — it is payables.** Receivable days actually *improved* from 116.5 to 103.4. But trade payables fell from ₹214.30 mn to ₹133.49 mn in FY24 (an ₴80.81 mn cash outflow, visible in the cash flow statement) and payable days collapsed from 168 to 99. **The Company de-stretched its creditors** — most visibly MSME dues, down from ₹49.18 mn to ₹21.42 mn (the 45-day MSMED payment rule). Good governance and lower supplier risk; **a permanent one-time cash cost and a structurally higher WC requirement going forward.**

**Receivables quality is good and improving:**

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross receivables >6 months | 35.89 | 14.26 | **12.40** |
| *as % of gross receivables* | *8.1%* | *3.8%* | ***2.6%*** |
| ECL provision | 8.36 | 10.10 | 13.77 |
| *as % of gross receivables* | *1.9%* | *2.7%* | *2.9%* |

Ageing improved materially (64% of the book "not due"), while the ECL provision was *raised* to 2.9% — a conservative combination. Credit-impaired receivables fell from ₹11.41 mn to ₹0.35 mn. The FY24 ₹16.03 mn bad-debt write-off appears to have cleaned out a legacy problem. **Provisioning at 74% expected-credit-rate on >365-day balances is appropriately harsh.**

**FX exposure is real and unhedged in substance:** FY25 USD trade receivables of $1.77 mn (₹150.73 mn) plus a USD FCNR term loan of $0.77 mn (₹65.56 mn). A ₹100 mn forward-contract facility is sanctioned but **nil outstanding.** Net USD long ~₹85 mn — a 1% INR move is ~₹0.85 mn of P&L. Manageable, but the natural hedge is being run open.

## 13. Disclosure quality — six specific findings

The audited financials are clean, tie out, and carry no qualification. These findings concern **presentation**, and they matter because they all point the same direction — flattering.

**① The industry report's EBITDA for the Company is wrong (double-deducts other income).**

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Company KPI EBITDA (p.124) | 313.27 | 225.51 | 357.88 |
| CARE report "Operating EBITDA" (p.170–171) | 277.19 | 190.86 | 304.61 |
| Difference | 36.08 | 34.65 | 53.27 |
| **Other income** | **36.08** | **34.65** | **53.25** |

The difference equals other income **exactly, in all three years.** The Company's definition already deducts other income (PBT + finance + D&A − other income = 357.88 ✅). The CARE table deducts it a second time. Proof it is an error: **CARE's own EBIT line (₹256.79 mn) is consistent with the Company's EBITDA of ₹357.88 mn, not with CARE's ₹304.61 mn** (304.61 − 101.07 = 203.54 ≠ 256.79). So the same document reports EBITDA margins of both **22.18% and 18.88%** for FY25. Investors reading the industry section understate profitability by 330–460 bps.

**② Top-5 customer concentration compared against a top-10 number.** Page 189 claims top-5 fell from 53.56% (FY23) to 26.00% (FY25). 53.56% is top-*ten*; actual FY23 top-5 = **39.58%**. Overstates the de-risking by ~2x.

**③ WACA appears not to be adjusted for the sub-division and bonus issue.** The DRHP discloses WACA of **₹16.42** from five secondary transactions. Reproducing it: (2,000 × ₹3,000 + 2,850,000 × ₹14.33) ÷ 2,852,000 = **₹16.42** — an exact match. But the four December-2024 transfers of 500 shares each were of **₹10 face value shares, before** the 6 Feb 2025 sub-division (₹10 → ₹5, ×2) and the 8 Feb 2025 bonus (×112). Each of those shares became **224 current shares**, so ₹3,000 becomes **₹13.39** per current share, and the count becomes 448,000, not 2,000. Properly adjusted: **WACA ≈ ₹14.20.** The disclosed figure appears **overstated by ~15.6%.** Since the RHP must express floor and cap price as a multiple of WACA, an inflated WACA makes the offer price look like a *lower* multiple of promoter cost. At ₹53, the multiple is 3.23x on the disclosed WACA versus **3.73x on the adjusted figure.**

**④ Capital expenditure stated two ways.** MD&A (p.320) states FY25/24/23 capex of ₹301.77 / ₹359.95 / ₹166.81 mn; the table immediately below shows ₹197.73 / ₹296.47 / ₹79.59 mn; the cash flow shows payments of ₹154.11 / ₹286.39 / ₹135.42 mn. Reconcilable (CWIP and capital advances), but three different numbers in adjacent paragraphs with no bridge.

**⑤ Aggregate capacity utilisation does not reconcile to the unit-level table** (Section 8) — reported utilisation is 3.6 pp (FY25) and 5.7 pp (FY24) above the year-end-capacity computation, with no averaging methodology disclosed.

**⑥ A 14.2% cumulative change described as a "CAGR of 14%"** (Hy-Tech-USA-sourced revenue, ₹279.85 → ₹319.59 mn over two years; true CAGR 6.9%).

Also noted: Pithampur-I capacity increase printed as "150,00%"; a garbled OCI line item in the P&L; "FINANCIAL INTEBTEDNESS" heading; "page 117117"; and a claim that the domestic network covered "three states" (p.121) versus "four states" (p.182).

**Conclusion:** no evidence of accounting manipulation — the audited statements are internally consistent and I could re-derive every subtotal. But **the narrative and marketing sections of this document are not carefully prepared, and each identified error runs in the issuer's favour.** For a first-time issuer with a sole small BRLM, apply a governance discount and verify any promotional statistic against the financial statements.

## 14. Valuation

### 14.1 Where the price band came from — a structural constraint, not just demand

This is a non-obvious point worth understanding. **The DRHP's offer structure was mathematically incapable of supporting a high price.**

Under SCRR Rule 19(2)(b), an issuer with post-issue capital below ₹1,600 cr must offer **at least 25% to the public.** With the DRHP structure (₹700 mn fresh + 11,933,120 OFS shares) on a pre-issue base of 83,531,840 shares:

| Price (₹) | Fresh shares | Post-issue shares | Public % |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 | 14,000,000 | 97,531,840 | 26.6% |
| **58.66** | **11,933,120** | **95,465,014** | **25.00%** |
| 70 | 10,000,000 | 93,531,840 | 23.4% ❌ |
| 100 | 7,000,000 | 90,531,840 | 20.9% ❌ |

**Above ₹58.66 the DRHP structure breached the 25% minimum public shareholding.** The RHP resolved it exactly as the arithmetic requires — **cutting the fresh issue to ₹600 mn and raising the OFS to ~14.29 mn shares**, landing the band at ₹50–53 for 27.0% public shareholding.

**Implication for clients: the ₹53 price is not primarily a statement that the promoters and BRLM believed the business was worth only ₹503 cr. It is the price the chosen offer size permitted.** That is precisely why the issue looks cheap against peers and why the GMP is 83% — the *structure*, not the *valuation judgement*, set the price. It also means the apparent "discount to peers" should not be read as a considered fairness signal.

**How to read this table — it is the core of my view.** The right-hand columns are *not* one-year targets; reaching 65–70% utilisation on the expanded base needs revenue up **48–60% from FY26**, which at the industry's 10.4% CAGR is a **3–5 year journey.**

- At the **issue price of ₹53**, you are paying below the 60%-utilisation / 12x value of ₹71. **You are not paying for the expansion at all.** That is the source of the upside.
- At the **GMP-implied ₹97**, you are paying above the **75%-utilisation / 12x value of ₹89**, and are relying on a 15x multiple *plus* full absorption. **The entire multi-year execution is pre-paid.**

A reasonable 3-year fair value, applying a 12x EV/EBITDA multiple (justified by 22% margins and 20%+ ROE, discounted for the related-party channel, cyclicality and disclosure quality) to a 65% utilisation outcome, is **₹75–85 per share.**

## 15. Risk register

### Structural / high severity
1. **Promoter-owned US channel — 22.9% of revenue (FY25).** 100% of US revenue flows through or is sourced by Hy-Tech USA Inc.; 100% of sales commission is paid to it. Value split outside the listed entity; permanent related-party dependency.
2. **Flat EBIT and falling ROIC on rising capital.** FY23→FY25 EBIT +1.9% on +21% revenue; ROIC 23.2% → 15.8%; gross block turnover −35%. The IPO adds more capital to this base.
3. **~60% capacity addition against 63–70% current utilisation.** Absorption is a 3–5 year task on market growth alone.
4. **No long-term customer contracts.** All business purchase-order based; top-10 = 42% of revenue.
5. **Cyclical, correlated end-markets.** Construction + farming + automotive = 52.3% of revenue; no counter-cyclical segment.
6. **Plating plant *Consent to Operate* not yet applied for.** Regulatory single point of failure sitting downstream of ~65% of the capex.
7. **US tariff exposure not addressed anywhere in the DRHP.** Section 232 metals tariffs and a standing CVD order on forged steel fittings from India post-date the document.

### Financial / moderate severity
8. **Cash conversion collapse:** CFO/EBITDA 99% → 47.5%; 3-year FCF/PAT of 18.5%.
9. **Working capital intensity rising:** core trade WC 34.6% of revenue; CCC 12 → 65 days; payables de-stretched permanently.
10. **~₹6 mn p.a. negative carry** on ₹172 mn of deposits versus 11.4% debt; reported current ratio of 2.21x becomes 1.83x excluding parked deposits.
11. **FY25 earnings flattered ~10%** by a ₹26.88 mn non-recurring asset-disposal gain (13.7% of PAT).
12. **No pricing power** — FY24 showed cost pass-through failure destroying 40% of EBIT on flat volumes.
13. **Unhedged net USD long ~₹85 mn** despite a ₹100 mn forward facility sitting unused.
14. **Steel input, no purchase agreements** — no contractual price protection.
15. **Promoter personal guarantees** secure ₹395 mn of debt; restrictive covenants on capital structure and management changes.

### Governance / disclosure
16. **Six identified disclosure errors, all flattering** (Section 13) — including a misstated WACA and an erroneous EBITDA table.
17. **Product mix revenue undisclosed;** end-market undisclosed for 39.8% of revenue (only 60.2% attributable); 4 of top-10 customers unnamed; **OEM-vs-distributor split within "direct sales" never given.**
18. **Sole BRLM** (New Berry Capitals) — limited distribution and price-discovery depth.
19. **~3% dividend payout;** no income expectation.
20. **Four of six plants in Maharashtra** (77.3% of revenue); domestic revenue 52.3% West-weighted.

### Mitigants worth weighing
✅ Clean audit, no qualifications or frauds since incorporation · negligible contingent liabilities (₹nil disputed GST at FY25) · **best EBITDA margin and ROE in the disclosed peer set** · 95.2% repeat revenue, 128 repeat customers · genuine backward integration (forging → machining → plating) · IATF 16949 at three plants · **39% margin of safety to operating break-even** · net debt/EBITDA 0.72x falling to ~0.2x post-issue · supplier concentration cut (top-5 55.3% → 38.5%) · customer concentration cut (top-10 53.6% → 42.0%) · receivables ageing materially improved with conservative ECL · debottlenecking-led (not greenfield) capex at running, licensed units · **FY26 delivered +17.4% revenue, +15.1% PAT, ~70% utilisation and the first real export growth in four years.**
